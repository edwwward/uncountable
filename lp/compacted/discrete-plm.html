THE COMMON CHALLENGE

Engineering changes shouldn't get lost in translation on their way to the rest of your organization

Manufacturers worldwide need a PLM system built for how engineering actually works, not email threads and shared drives, and not legacy PLM systems straining to keep up. Sound familiar?

Engineers live in CAD, eBOMs synced by hand

Every export, re-key, and version check is another chance for the BOM and CAD model to drift apart, and redesigns often restart from scratch when the original rationale can't be found.

Change control still happens over email

Approvals, redlines, and sign-offs get buried in threads instead of living on the record they affect, stalling the handoff to manufacturing while specs sit in one engineer's inbox.

Manufacturing, engineering, and quality finally share one system

Specs, BOMs, and released documents live in the same platform manufacturing already works in, so the production floor is always pulling the current revision instead of a copy someone emailed last quarter.

No standardized process across sites

Without a standardized process across sites, engineering, quality, and manufacturing defer to their own version, causing inconsistency and loss of critical information.

The program and the product, on one timeline

Discrete PLM isn't only the part and the BOM, it's the program that gets them to launch. Uncountable plans and gates that program natively, so schedule, approvals, and engineering data never live in separate tools.

  • Gantt timelines track workstreams, dependencies, and milestones, along with resourcing and spend; helping indicate impact instantly
  • Configurable stage gates move programs from concept to launch, with approvals at each step; the same engine gates ECOs
  • Configurable intake routes requests, like tolerance issues or new parts, to a clear owner with status tracking
